# History
Magic was always a rather difficult discipline to learn. For the longest time, nobody could even prove magic existed!
Surely, there were wondrous feats people did throughout history, but everyone thought it to be folklore. In the later half of the 18th century, however, the Age of Enlightenment began. Academic fields of all kinds advanced in giant leaps. Every university scrambled to get the next biggest proof about mathematics, physics, astronomy, philosophy... and amidst all that, the existence of magic was proven. What started as a rumor and quickly took off as the next big field in academia, proved to be a dud.
It would take a couple years, until Émilie du Châtelet wrote in her Institutions physiques about the aether suffusing our world.
While her original assumption, that there was simply not enough aether on earth because God was so far away, was never proven, the problem about not enough aether existing was true. Humans, Elves, Dwarves, Goblins, Lizardfolk, or Ravenfolk simply cannot channel enough aether - or mana, only a part of aether - in their bodies. Émilie mused it would require a group of magically gifted people, to produce substantial effects. She managed to gather interested idividuals in the Café Gradot. A gathering place for intellectuals of the time.
Her idea to combine the inner reserves of everyone present via complex mathematical vectors worked out. Having half a dozen people go unconscious from severe pain over lighting a lantern without opening its lid, was just not the outcome she had hoped for.
In general, as understanding of magic slowly increased, it was found out, the training and dedication needed to make small parlor tricks happen, was just not worth it. Conjuring a small light to work longer sounded very useful - if only you wouldn't need more than 2 people to not pass out immediately, or at least require medical attention immediately. And that was only IF the complex diagrams needed for pooled magic were correct.
With the industrial revolution interest in magic all but vanished. Instead most of the economy was focused on advancing engineering, machining, and whatever else would actually increase productivity - or make it easier to defend one's country. Every once in a while a new student would come up with a theoretically possible spell. Or a new theory about the nature of magic. But in the end, if you didn't train for at least a decade, even just reading a scroll could cause an intense headache.

At the time of WWI there was an attempt to get any use out of magic, no matter how small. Sending messages, conjuring rations, even igniting a fuse in harsh weather could be deciding factors in the trenches. But at the end it was just another futile waste of resources. Corps XXXX of the Ottoman Empire was supposed be the secret deciding factor for the War. But when the war ended, the most they had achieved, was igniting a fuse in ideal circumstances with only a major headache, and being able to move after only 5 minutes of rest. A great accomplishment in the field of magical research, but ultimately proof that magic would stay a hobby for intellectuals who already had everything in life.

Until recently you only really learned about magic if it was you special interest, you had ideological interest in it, or via one of those shows that were usually occupied with various esoteric theories about how only aliens could explain earlier high civilizations on earth. A certain Prof. Bremman probably the most prominent figure in them. He was the one who found out that mana and Æthereum are not technically the same. Mana is but an aspect of the Æthereum. And Æthereum is more like a force. A force that runs in streams across the globe. Historical assumptions about Leylines turned out correct, or so he claims. He is also sturbbornly defending his mathematical evidences of a "bubble" of sorts, is surrounding our entire universe. And it is, of course, made out of Æthereum. Appearing in any interview, or ridiculous talk show that will invite him, he seems rather annoyed talking about though, because his *unproven* theory claims, that there is something outside that Æthereum. Something opposite of Æthereum, he calls Nethereum. Supposedly it's what prevents us from using magic in a better way, as we need tiny bits of Nethereum for all magic applications. And even more of it for all kinds of spatial applications, like teleportation or similar effects.

Which is interesting, because teleportation is probably one of the best researched applications of magic. Apparently, once you become a billionaire, it is tradition to hire every competent or dedicated magic enthusiast near you, and make them research teleportation magic. When it first started, and it was some giant logistics company CEO, it made sense. But then car company CEOs or CEOs of large videogame publishing companies did the same. And if their social media posts are anything to trust, they can teleport small boxes somewhat large distances, or large boxes small distances. Every couple of months they brag about it, followed by silence and stocks for painkillers rising.

And that was the state of magic more or less unchanged until a year ago. Those teleportation tries? Something must have gone wrong. Quickly deleted posts claimed fire and military around what was largely understood as "wizard towers" by the public. It went quiet for a bit before Prof. Bremman went viral with a privately hosted podcast about how he was right all along. He claimed he didn't have access to the equipment anymore, but was sure that Nethereum crossed over into our world. His proof: Large gestures, incoherent speech, and his hands got encased in stone. Cheap special effects, even free ai could do in real time. But apparently it was true.

All over the world, magic enthusiasts demanded to finally be called "wizards" as they could cool down their beer to ice blocks in mere seconds. "Technomancers" went viral for making a popular virtual singer appear next to them, reportedly only feeling 'drained' but not any more tired in any way. Small communities got together to practice magic together. The government tried to secretly hire the most promising ones - which got whistleblowed a day later by someone who figured out a spell to enhance their hearing. Apparently those historical scrolls kept solely for record keeping also mostly worked. Not all of them were correctly written down, but allegedly most of them worked. It was hard to test however, as most libraries, museums and other collections got closed to the public, and you needed special clearance to even see them.
Quickly spells got classified into legal, restricted, and illegal spells by the MAGIC Act.
Legal spells were just for ease of life, useful to an everyday citizen. Spells that could clean your clothes on the go, let you know the rough shape of a forest, make walking easier, or let you know current date a time - a laughably replaceable spell.
Restricted Spells would have made public security a nightmare at least to a degree. Spells that could open locks, cause pain of some sort, or interfered with public infrastructure...
And finally illegal spells were not further explained to the public. But as long as the electrical grid was still up, you could see people in all parts of the world use their rural surrounding for target practice to create huge explosions out of nothing, have lightning impact a tree in the middle of a sunny day, or freeze a large tank filled with water solid so quickly it shattered.
Small businesses started selling legal spells. And since it was already known, they came with small flyers leading you to a .gov page to let you apply to server your country.

But the peaceful practicing of magic soon turned grim, when the government couldn't quite keep it under wraps that Nethereum wasn't the only thing that crossed over. Something colloquially called "The Blob" was oozing out of revived corpses - or Zombies. Mixed reactions turned into panic when it wouldn't stop spreading. What started as small groups of likeminded individuals turned into magical circles. But the little time we had, wasn't enough to stop the Blob. Much less what came a year after the Æthereum got pierced.

# Classes

 There are two different ways to tap into magic: you can exert your will on the Æthereum (Control), or you can be a conduit for Æthereum to exert its will (Harmony). These two ways are polar opposites of each other, and when they are used for the first time (during the studying process, as you of course have to try to cast the spell to learn it) change the mage's body irrevocably. This is fundamental to why each mage class has an opposing class.

The 8 mage classes are: Magus and Animist, Kelvinist and Stormshaper, Technomancer and Earthshaper, Biomancer and Druid. The first class in the pair is Control and the second is Harmony. The interesting thing here is you can choose between Control and Harmony for each pair, instead of having to stick to one for all classes, which is a subject of study on its own.

In the case of "how is a spell part of a class?" the spell's characteristics determine the class; the class does not determine the spells. The classes were established in ancient Greece, when it was noted that certain spells were incompatible with knowing other spells. So whenever a spell was invented, the universe would "decide" what class it is.

### Class Characteristics

- Magus: Force and Waves. This can be described as Time, Light, Gravity, and Force. Magus spells are the most versatile of the classes, and it also usually has the more difficult to learn spells. It is the class that has the most impact on the fundamental laws of the universe.
- Animist: Spirits. As Magus is the physical universe, animist is the metaphysical. Usually misunderstood and as such has a bit of a stigma throughout history as necromancers. This class also features blood magic, which is another reason it has a stigma.
- Kelvinist: Heat and Cold spells. Can also produce light to a lesser degree.
- Stormshaper: Wind, weather, and electricity. Most of these spells are really loud.
- Technomancer: Invention. This usually manifests as spells that summon arms and armor, holograms, tools, and buffs that focus on the human condition. The Technomancer rune is also uniquely versatile, allowing for much wider ability for magic to work with mundane inventions. Technomancer became a lot more popular with the invention of electricity, even while magic as a whole declined, as it has a finer control of electricity than Stormshaper, though it does not have the power.
- Earthshaper: Stone, dirt, geography, things to do with the earth. The spells often focus on melee and defense. Lava is a high-tier Earthshaper thing as well. This class features spells that use stamina as their energy source.
- Biomancer: Flesh mage. Manipulating the caster's flesh is easier but higher tier spells can manipulate others' flesh. Features Acid.
- Druid: Plant mage. Communes with nature. The typical "druid" of old is usually a combination of this class and Earthshaper. Has a slight secondary affinity with water.


With the classes above listed, you might notice that there is some overlap. This overlap actually results in spells that are "classless," due to sharing one more traits with multiple classes that do not tip the scales in one direction or another. These spells are overwhelmingly weak spells, as stronger spells inevitably have characteristics that will tip the scales, whether they are obvious or not.
